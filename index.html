Context & Objectives

Project Overview

Understanding customer attrition economics, business problem formulation, and supervised machine learning methodology.

What is Customer Churn?

Customer churn (or customer attrition) refers to the percentage of subscribers who terminate their service contracts or discontinue subscriptions with a telecom service provider during a given time frame.

In contract-based and recurring subscription models, reducing churn is the single most critical factor in sustaining long-term top-line revenue and profitability.

Why Churn is Vital in Telecom

Acquiring a new telecom subscriber is 5 to 7 times more expensive than retaining an existing customer due to steep customer acquisition costs (CAC) such as advertising, hardware subsidies, and technician installation.

Even a modest 5% reduction in customer churn can increase telecom operating profit margins by 25% to 85% by extending customer lifetime value (LTV).

The Business Problem

Telecom operators frequently operate reactively—engaging customers only after they contact customer care to cancel. By then, the cancellation decision is already finalized.

The challenge is to identify leading risk indicators early in the customer lifecycle, allowing proactive retention campaigns before customers experience churn fatigue.

Project Objectives

  • Perform end-to-end Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) on 7,043 subscriber records.
  • Discover key empirical churn drivers using Gini feature importance.
  • Train an interpretable, white-box Decision Tree Classifier for transparent decision rules.
  • Develop actionable behavioral customer personas for segmented retention.
  • Deliver an interactive prediction engine and dynamic risk dashboard for decision support.

Machine Learning Approach

Unlike complex black-box ensemble methods, this project deliberately employs an interpretable Decision Tree Classifier.

Decision trees generate clear, human-understandable `IF-THEN` conditional rules that business executives and frontline customer service agents can directly inspect, audit, and operationalize without machine learning opacity.

Expected Business Benefits

  • Proactive Outreach: Engage at-risk subscribers 30–60 days before contract expiry.
  • Targeted Retention Spending: Allocate promotional discounts exclusively to vulnerable, high-value cohorts.
  • Reduced Waste: Prevent over-discounting loyal customers who are already stable.
  • Optimized Onboarding: Streamline initial 90-day onboarding to mitigate early lifecycle attrition.

Numerical Variables (3)

Quantitative continuous measurements reflecting subscriber lifecycle and financial volume:

  • tenure: Total number of months the customer has been subscribed (Range: 0 to 72 months, Mean: 32.37 mos, Median: 29.0 mos).
  • MonthlyCharges: Current monthly invoice amount billed to the customer (Range: $18.25 to $118.75, Mean: $64.76).
  • TotalCharges: Cumulative lifetime amount charged (Range: $18.80 to $8,684.80, Mean: $2,279.73).

Categorical & Demographic Variables (4)

Customer demographic background attributes:

  • gender: Female (49.5%) / Male (50.5%).
  • SeniorCitizen: Binary indicator for customers aged 65 and above (16.2% Seniors).
  • Partner: Whether the subscriber has a spouse/partner (48.3% Yes).
  • Dependents: Whether the subscriber lives with dependents/children (29.9% Yes).

Customer Service-Related Variables (9)

Active telecom subscriptions and security/utility add-on products:

  • PhoneService & MultipleLines: Landline phone connectivity.
  • InternetService: DSL (34.4%), Fiber optic (44.0%), or No Internet (21.6%).
  • OnlineSecurity & OnlineBackup: Cybersecurity and cloud data backup utilities.
  • DeviceProtection & TechSupport: Hardware insurance and technical helpdesk.
  • StreamingTV & StreamingMovies: Digital entertainment packages.

Billing & Target Variables (5)

Contractual commitment terms, billing channels, and primary target label:

  • Contract: Month-to-month (55.0%), One year (20.9%), Two year (24.1%).
  • PaperlessBilling: Electronic statement delivery (59.2% Yes).
  • PaymentMethod: Electronic check (33.6%), Mailed check (22.9%), Bank transfer (21.9%), Credit card (21.6%).
  • Churn (Target Variable): Binary ground truth (Yes [26.54%] vs No [73.46%]).

Decision Tree Feature Weights
Key Finding: Contract_Month-to-month accounts for 51.75% of the total tree split purity gain, followed by InternetService_Fiber optic (16.44%) and tenure (15.50%). Together, these top 3 drivers explain over 83.6% of predictive variation.

Association vs. Causality

Methodological Clarification: While statistical models reveal strong associations (e.g. Electronic check users having 45.3% churn), paying by electronic check may not directly cause churn; rather, it often reflects uncommitted or cash-flow sensitive subscribers. Telecom interventions must address root pain points (e.g. invoice transparency, auto-pay incentives) rather than treating indicators as isolated causes.

Empirical Synthesis

Project Findings

Major quantitative discoveries, empirical churn patterns, and verified model insights from the 7,043 subscriber dataset.

1. Contract Lock-in is the #1 Retention Shield

Subscribers on Month-to-month contracts account for 88.5% (1,655 of 1,869) of all churned customers, experiencing a 42.71% attrition rate compared to only 11.27% for 1-year and 2.83% for 2-year contracts.

2. Year-1 Onboarding is the Critical Window

Over 55.5% of total churn occurs within the first 12 months of tenure (47.44% first-year churn rate). Once a subscriber stays past 36 months, retention climbs drastically to over 83.2%.

3. Fiber Optic Requires Support Bundling

Fiber optic users churn at 41.89% (vs 18.96% for DSL). However, adding Technical Support and Online Security slashes fiber churn down to 15.17%, proving that high-speed broadband requires active support buffering.

4. Automated Billing Cuts Churn by 65%

Manual Electronic Check payment is associated with 45.29% churn. Transitioning customers to automated card or bank payments drops attrition to 15.24% by eliminating recurring payment reconsideration friction.

5. White-Box Decision Tree Transparency

The trained Decision Tree Classifier achieves 79.84% accuracy and 0.8281 ROC-AUC, proving that high interpretability and transparent rule extraction can be achieved without sacrificing predictive power.

6. Persona-Driven Retention Maximizes ROI

Targeting distinct customer archetypes (e.g. New Month-to-Month with contract discounts and High-Charge with bundled perks) delivers higher retention ROI than blunt, unsegmented promotional spending.

How interpretable machine learning and persona profiling empower telecom organizations to transition from reactive firefighting to proactive customer retention.

Transforming Telecom Churn Management through Explainable AI

Customer churn remains one of the most significant threats to profitability in the subscription telecommunications sector. This academic Data Science case study demonstrates that customer attrition is not random; rather, it follows identifiable behavioral pathways driven primarily by contract flexibility, early onboarding friction, pricing thresholds, and support availability.

By integrating an interpretable Decision Tree Classifier (79.84% accuracy, 0.8281 ROC-AUC) with 4 actionable behavioral personas, telecom operators gain the dual advantage of high predictive accuracy and complete algorithmic transparency. Frontline agents and commercial leaders can understand exactly why a subscriber is flagged as at-risk and apply pre-approved, persona-specific retention playbooks before the customer initiates cancellation.
